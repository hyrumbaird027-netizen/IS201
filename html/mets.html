Jazz is a music genre born in the early 20th century in New Orleans, rooted in a fusion of African American musical traditions — blues, ragtime, and gospel — with European harmonic structures. It's defined by improvisation, syncopated rhythms, and a swinging feel that gives it a sense of spontaneity and conversation between musicians. From its New Orleans origins it spread rapidly, evolving through distinct eras: the big band swing of the 1930s, bebop's complex harmonies in the 40s, cool jazz, hard bop, and eventually the free jazz and fusion movements of the 60s and 70s. What sets jazz apart is its emphasis on individual expression within a collective framework. A jazz performance is never quite the same twice — musicians listen and respond to each other in real time, bending melodies, reharmonizing chords, and trading phrases in a kind of musical dialogue. This interplay between structure and freedom has made jazz one of the most influential genres in music history, shaping rock, R&B, hip-hop, and contemporary classical music alike, while continuing to evolve in the hands of new generations of artists.   3. Technical Proficiency

  4. Theory Knowledge
    • Understanding harmony and voice leading
    • Common jazz forms (blues, rhythm changes)
  5. Listening
    • Studying the masters
    • Playing along with recordings
